# Literature checks (2026-11-27)

`confirmed: true` means the bibliographic data were seen in Crossref or the publisher's own metadata. "Finding" is a one-sentence summary taken from the abstract where one was available, and the source of each finding is given. Nothing here was taken from memory without a flag.

## Pierson (1996)
- **Reference:** Pierson, Paul (1996). "The New Politics of the Welfare State". *World Politics* 48(2), 143-179. doi:10.1353/wp.1996.0004.
- **Confirmed:** true (Crossref).
- **Finding:** Retrenchment follows a different logic from expansion. Politicians try to avoid blame for cutting programmes, and mature programmes create constituencies that defend them. Together these explain why welfare states proved so resilient during two decades of austerity. The evidence comes from Britain, the United States, Germany and Sweden.
- **Source of finding:** the abstract, via Crossref.

## Saint-Paul (2000)
- **Reference:** Saint-Paul, Gilles (2000). *The Political Economy of Labour Market Institutions*. Oxford: Oxford University Press. ISBN 0198293321 / 9780198293323. doi:10.1093/0198293321.001.0001.
- **Confirmed:** true (Crossref, OUP).
- **Finding:** Rigid labour market institutions are the outcome of a political process dominated by employed workers with intermediate skills (the insiders). Reform is difficult because of status-quo bias, but an appropriate design or timing can still make it possible.
- **Source of finding:** the OUP abstract.

## Vis (2010)
- **Reference:** Vis, Barbara (2010). *Politics of Risk-taking: Welfare State Reform in Advanced Democracies*. Amsterdam: Amsterdam University Press. ISBN 9789089642271. doi:10.5117/9789089642271.
- **Confirmed:** true (Crossref; AUP is the publisher, Amsterdam the place). Reviews give the length as 247 or 248 pages.
- **Finding:** The book draws on prospect theory. Governments accept the electoral risk of unpopular reforms, such as benefit cutbacks, mainly when they face losses: a deteriorating socio-economic situation or a deteriorating political position. When they face gains, they steer clear. The method is fuzzy-set QCA.
- **Source of finding:** search snippets of the publisher description and reviews (Taylor-Gooby, *Journal of Social Policy* 40(1), 2011). The book itself was not read, so the finding is `partly` confirmed.

## Duval, Furceri and Miethe (2021)
- **Is the cited journal correct?** **False.** Crossref has no article by these three authors in the *European Economic Review* (ISSN 0014-2921). Their 2021 paper is in a different journal.
- **Correct reference:** Duval, Romain; Furceri, Davide; Miethe, Jakob (2021). "Robust political economy correlates of major product and labor market reforms in advanced economies: Evidence from BAMLE for logit models". *Journal of Applied Econometrics* 36(1), 98-124 (online 5 Oct 2020). doi:10.1002/jae.2791.
- **Earlier version:** "The Needle in the Haystack: What Drives Labor and Product Market Reforms in Advanced Countries?", IMF Working Paper 18/101 (2018), doi:10.5089/9781484353639.001.
- **Possible source of the confusion:** Duval and Furceri did publish in the *European Economic Review* in 2021: Cacciatore, Duval, Furceri and Zdzienicka, "Fiscal multipliers and job-protection regulation", EER 132, 103616. Its topic is different.
- **Finding:** High unemployment and economic crises are robust correlates of reform, which supports the crisis-induces-reform hypothesis. Other robust correlates are:
  - reform convergence: countries with tighter regulation are more likely to liberalise;
  - reforms in other countries;
  - formal pressure to reform.
  Political factors matter most for job-protection reforms, and international pressure matters most for product-market reforms.
- **Source of finding:** the JAE abstract, via Crossref.

## Ross (2000)
- **Reference:** Ross, Fiona (2000). "Interests and choice in the 'not quite so new' politics of welfare". *West European Politics* 23(2), 11-34. doi:10.1080/01402380008425365.
- **Confirmed:** true (Crossref).
- **Finding:** Ross argues that the new-politics literature has done much to show retrenchment, and to a lesser extent reformulation, but remains "overly de-politicised". Partisan interests and political choice still shape welfare reform. A Taylor & Francis book chapter with the same title exists (doi:10.4324/9781315039916-2), probably a reprint of the journal issue as a book. The volume was not identified.
- **Source of finding:** fragments of the abstract in search snippets (ACNP catalogue, Taylor & Francis). The full abstract was not retrieved, so the finding is `partly` confirmed.

## Hassel and Schiller (2010)
- **Reference:** Hassel, Anke; Schiller, Christof (2010). *Der Fall Hartz IV. Wie es zur Agenda 2010 kam und wie es weitergeht*. Frankfurt am Main / New York: Campus. 348 pp. ISBN 978-3-593-39336-0.
- **Confirmed:**
  - Title and authors are true, from the review by Helge F. Jani in *PVS* 53(1), 2012, 146-148, doi:10.5771/0032-3470-2012-1-146, via Crossref.
  - Publisher, place, pages and ISBN are `partly` confirmed. They come from the DNB/EconBiz record seen only as a search snippet, and from Campus's Leseprobe file named 9783593393360. The EconBiz page itself was blocked by a bot check.
  - The second author is spelled "Christof".
- **Finding:** The book is a history of how Hartz IV came about, based on more than fifty interviews with the decision-makers involved. Hassel's companion article in *PVS* 51(1), 2010, 95-117 gives the argument: Hartz IV was a response to the crisis of municipal finances, as social assistance costs were shifted under fiscal federalism. That article is "Sozialpolitik im Finanzföderalismus – Hartz IV als Antwort auf die Krise der Kommunalfinanzen" (Crossref).
- **Source of finding:** the review snippet and the article title. Both are `partly` confirmed.

## Wlezien (2005)
- **Which paper is meant?** This is ambiguous. The most likely match for a "Wlezien (2005)" citation in this context is:
  - Wlezien, Christopher (2005). "On the salience of political issues: The problem with 'most important problem'". *Electoral Studies* 24(4), 555-579. doi:10.1016/j.electstud.2005.01.009.
  - **Confirmed:** true (Crossref).
- **Finding:** The standard "most important problem" survey item mixes the importance of an issue with the degree to which it is a problem. It is therefore a poor measure of issue salience.
- **Source of finding:** search snippets and later literature that cites it (for example Jennings and Wlezien 2011). The 2005 abstract text was not retrieved, so the finding is `partly` confirmed.
- **Other candidate:** Soroka and Wlezien (2005), "Opinion-policy dynamics: public preferences and public expenditure in the United Kingdom", *British Journal of Political Science*. This was **not checked**. Check which work the manuscript means.

## Hochmuth, Kohlbrecher, Merkl and Gartner (2021)
- **Reference:** Hochmuth, Brigitte; Kohlbrecher, Britta; Merkl, Christian; Gartner, Hermann (2021). "Hartz IV and the decline of German unemployment: A macroeconomic evaluation". *Journal of Economic Dynamics and Control* 127, 104114 (June 2021). doi:10.1016/j.jedc.2021.104114.
- **Confirmed:** true (Crossref and the IAB page).
- **Earlier versions:** IAB-Discussion Paper 3/2019, CESifo WP 8933, FAU Discussion Paper 01/2019 and IZA DP 12260.

### Abstract, verbatim
From the English IAB-Doku page, [iab.de/en/publications/publication/?id=11451682](https://iab.de/en/publications/publication/?id=11451682), which is the author's abstract. The JEDC page on ScienceDirect was blocked (403):

> "This paper proposes a new approach to evaluate the macroeconomic effects of the "Hartz IV" reform, which reduced the generosity of long-term unemployment benefits. We propose a model with different unemployment durations, where the reform initiates both a partial effect and an equilibrium effect. We estimate the relative importance of these two effects and the size of the partial effect based on the IAB Job Vacancy Survey. Our approach does not hinge on an external source for the decline in the replacement rate for long-term unemployed. **We find that Hartz IV was a major driver for the decline of Germany's steady state unemployment and that partial and equilibrium effect were of equal importance.** In addition, we provide direct empirical evidence on labor selection, one potential dimension of recruiting intensity."

### IAB summary, verbatim
From the German IAB page, [iab.de/publikationen/publikation/?id=11451682](https://iab.de/publikationen/publikation/?id=11451682) (Autorenreferat, IAB-Doku):

> "Unser Ergebnis ist, dass die Hartz IV Reform **eine wichtige Ursache** für die seit 2005 sinkende Arbeitslosigkeit in Deutschland darstellt, wobei der Partialeffekt und der Gleichgewichtseffekt etwa gleichbedeutend waren."

### Precise characterisation
- The paper calls Hartz IV "**a major driver**" of the decline in **steady-state** unemployment. The German summary says "eine wichtige Ursache", an important cause. Neither text says it is *the* main or sole cause.
- The partial and equilibrium effects are of equal importance.

### Magnitude
- The 2019 discussion paper version reports that the calibrated model implies the reform "caused the German unemployment rate to drop by 2.2 percentage points". Source: [IAB-DP 3/2019](https://doku.iab.de/discussionpapers/2019/dp0319.pdf), p. 5.
- In a robustness check the effect is 1.7 points.
- The paper also cites a range in the literature from 0.1 points (Launov/Wälde 2013) to 2.8 points (Krause/Uhlig 2012).
- This number was **not verified in the JEDC version**.
- A search snippet of an IZA Newsroom piece gave "more than two percentage points ... roughly one million" jobs. That page was blocked and is unverified.

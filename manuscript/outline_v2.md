## Core thesis (draft)

Germany's slump has reached the labour market roughly as much as the output gap predicts. What sets it apart is where the rest of the loss went. Of a shortfall of almost ten percent against the pre-2019 trend, about half is now booked as lost potential output, not as slack. The shock behind that write-down is real and largely supply-side: an energy-price level shift and Chinese competition in Germany's core industries. What the buffers do is hide it. Demographic scarcity, a narrow sectoral shock and a credible welfare backstop let firms absorb the loss through shorter hours rather than dismissals, so a structural decline reaches households as calm. Wolfgang Streeck described a German state that bought time with money; this one buys time with demography and working hours. The signal does not vanish. It arrives in the wrong currency: as a cultural grievance and a vote for the AfD, concentrated where the buffers do not reach, rather than as a demand for economic adjustment.

## Sections (word budget)

1. **The puzzle, restated** (700). Köcher's 7 percent; unemployment rose about as Okun predicts; the striking number is the potential write-down. Two gaps: output against potential (small) and output against trend (large). Streeck's *Buying Time* introduced as the frame.
2. **Not new, intensified** (900). Table 1 harmonised (one BA rate concept, gap column, peak-to-trough window). Figure 2 rebuilt on gaps: pass-through 2022–25 sits inside the pre-2009 band; 2009 and 2020 are the outliers. The novelty is how the loss is split between gap and potential, not the size of the unemployment response.
3. **How a supply shock stays invisible** (1,300). The shock: energy prices, China, export decoupling. The concealment: hours per worker −2.9 %, employment +1.5 %, energy-intensive output −15 % against jobs −6 %. Three buffers, ranked as a hypothesis. Measurement caveat: production-function estimates partly book hoarded labour as lower potential, so the write-down may be overstated.
4. **The growth model in reverse** (450). Baccaro and Pontusson: the institutions that coordinated export-led expansion now coordinate an orderly retreat. East Germany as precedent: transfers sustain incomes, not the productive base (about 200 words).
5. **Who the buffers protect** (550). Dualisation (Palier and Thelen; Emmenegger et al.; Hassel): insiders keep jobs at shorter hours; entrants, fixed-term and supplier workers and the energy-intensive regions carry the adjustment.
6. **The signal in the wrong currency** (1,000). Why the loss produces no reflationary or reform mandate: salience, veto points, fragmentation. Where the signal goes instead: AfD strength where the unprotected margin concentrates. March 2025 as fiscal expansion under an exiting majority. Streeck revisited: what happens when bought time runs out and the bill is cultural, not fiscal.
7. **Three clocks** (550). Demographic, fiscal, capability; the demographic buffer fails into output, not into unemployment.
8. **What follows and how to test it** (550). Three principles instead of German instrument lists. Two tests: (a) economic: potential estimates are revised up and the gap widens, so the slack was cyclical after all; (b) political: a structural reform passes through ordinary electoral politics. Check (b) against the 2025–26 legislation (Grundsicherung, pensions, planning).

Ending stays with Köcher: the calmest job-security reading on record is what this crisis looks like.

# Outline v2: Lost in Transmission (Phenomenal World, ca. 6,000 words)

Stand 23.11.2026. Kernthese nach Befund 1 und den Potenzialbelegen (`results/`).

## Core thesis (draft)

Germany's slump has reached the labour market roughly as much as the output gap predicts. What sets it apart is where the rest of the loss went. Of a shortfall of almost ten percent against the pre-2019 trend, about half is now booked as lost potential output, not as slack. Firms carried their workforces through the downturn by cutting hours rather than jobs, and what looks like a cyclical problem from inside the firm reads as a structural one in the national accounts. Three buffers make this possible: demographic scarcity, a narrow sectoral shock and a credible welfare backstop. Their political effect is the essay's real subject. A loss of potential produces no unemployment queue, no recession narrative and no constituency for adjustment. The recession-to-reform channel goes quiet, because the loss never takes the form the channel was built to carry.

## Sections (word budget)

1. **The puzzle, restated** (700). Köcher's 7 percent; unemployment rose about as Okun predicts; the striking number is the potential write-down. Two gaps: output against potential (small) and output against trend (large).
2. **Not new, intensified** (900). Table 1 harmonised (one BA rate concept, gap column, peak-to-trough window). Figure 2 rebuilt on gaps: pass-through 2022–25 sits inside the pre-2009 band; 2009 and 2020 are the outliers. The novelty is the split between gap and potential, not the size of the unemployment response.
3. **How a shock becomes lost potential** (1,400). Hours, not heads: hours per worker −2.9 %, employment +1.5 %, energy-intensive output −15 % against jobs −6 %. Three buffers, ranked as a hypothesis. Measurement caveat: production-function estimates book hoarded labour as lower TFP, so part of the potential loss is the buffer itself.
4. **Who the buffers protect** (600). Dualisation (Palier and Thelen; Emmenegger et al.; Hassel): insiders keep jobs at shorter hours; entrants, fixed-term and supplier workers carry the adjustment.
5. **Why no signal reaches politics** (1,000). Supply-side losses produce no recession narrative; salience, veto points, fragmentation; March 2025 as fiscal expansion under an exiting majority. Positioning against Streeck (2014): time bought with demography and hours rather than money.
6. **Three clocks** (700). Demographic, fiscal, capability; the demographic buffer fails into output, not into unemployment.
7. **What follows and how to test it** (700). Three principles instead of German instrument lists. Falsification: (a) potential estimates are revised up and the gap widens, so the slack was cyclical after all; (b) a structural reform passes through ordinary electoral politics; check the 2025–26 legislation (Grundsicherung, pensions, planning).

Ending stays with Köcher: the calmest job-security reading on record is what this crisis looks like.
